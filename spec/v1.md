# Hikkari v1 wire (v1.01, v1.02, v1.03, v1.04, v1.05)

Status: v1.01 deployed 2026-11-08 on one relay; v1.02 (duplicated UDP) 2026-11-09; v1.03 (lanes) 2026-11-09. v1 changes nothing on the wire of either channel against Naive and Hysteria2; what is Hikkari is the composition: one credential, one host, the channel choice and the fallback.

## Credential and link

One access key per user, the same on both channels.

```
hikkari+v1://hikkari:<key>@<address>:<port>?sni=<domain>#<label>
```

`address` may be an IP; `sni` is the relay's domain, which both channels present a certificate for (one ACME certificate). `hikkari://` is not used: it is the app's deep-link scheme.

## Stream channel (tcp/<port>)

Naive as `klzgrad/naiveproxy` speaks it: TLS by Chromium's network stack, HTTP/2 `CONNECT host:port`, `proxy-authorization: Basic base64("hikkari:<key>")`, the `padding` header and Naive padding of the first 8 reads and writes each way. A request without a valid credential is served the relay's site (probe resistance).

UDP-over-TCP: `CONNECT sp.v2.udp-over-tcp.arpa:0` opens a UDP association inside the tunnel, framed as sing's UoT v2: a request (`is_connect` byte, SOCKS address), then packets (`SOCKS address`, `uint16 length`, payload; the address is omitted in connect mode).

## Datagram channel (udp/<port>)

The Hysteria2 wire: QUIC v1 with the relay certificate and ALPN `h3`; the client authenticates by an HTTP/3 `POST https://hysteria/auth` with `Hysteria-Auth: <key>`; status 233 admits. Any other request, or a wrong key, gets the relay's site over HTTP/3. UDP rides QUIC DATAGRAM frames (session id, packet id, fragment id/count, SOCKS-style address, payload); a TCP stream opens with a Hysteria2 TCP request. Congestion control is BBR on both sides; the client's bandwidth claim is ignored.

Optional obfuscation (`udp.obfs`, salamander): every packet XORed with a keystream from BLAKE2b(password, salt). Off by default: random-looking UDP beside an HTTPS site is a signal of its own, while QUIC with the site's certificate looks like the site's HTTP/3.

## Client behaviour

- TCP: stream channel only.
- UDP: datagram channel, one attempt bounded by `connect_timeout` (4 s); on failure UDP goes over the stream channel for `retry_after` (60 s), then the datagram channel is tried again. The caller's own cancellation does not count as a failure. A network change forgets the failure.
- A dead datagram channel never affects TCP.

## Relay behaviour

- Both channels admit the same roster; bytes of both count into the same per-key totals; revoking a key ends live sessions on both.
- Exit policy applies to both: refused networks (private, the relay's own addresses), refused zones, refused TCP ports (25).
- A UDP flow without packets ends after 600 s (a muted call pauses ICE consent checks).

## v1.02: duplicated UDP

The link carries `dup=1` when the relay serves it; a client without it, or a relay without it, keeps v1.01.

```
hikkari+v1://hikkari:<key>@<address>:<port>?sni=<domain>&dup=1#<label>
```

Every UDP association of the application is one session (16 random bytes, the client's choice), carried by the datagram channel and, while the flow looks like a call or the datagram channel is down, by the stream channel at once. Each direction numbers its packets from 1; each end delivers the first copy of a number and drops the rest (a window of 1024 numbers). The relay keeps one exit socket per session, so the far end sees one address whichever channel a packet came by.

Frame (package `dup`):

| Field | Size | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| version | 1 | 1 |
| kind | 1 | 0 packet, 1 attach |
| session | 16 | |
| seq | 8 | big endian; 0 in an attach |
| address | SOCKS address + port | packet only: destination going up, source coming down |
| payload | rest | packet only |
| lane | 1 | attach only, optional (v1.03): 0 every packet, 1 voice; 0 when absent |

- Datagram channel: one frame per UDP message of a Hysteria2 UDP session addressed to `dup.hikkari.arpa:0`.
- Stream channel: `CONNECT dup.hikkari.arpa:0` through the Naive tunnel (same auth and padding), then frames, each after a `uint16` big endian length.
- A carrier joins a session with its first frame; the client sends an attach first. Answers go to every carrier joined. A session belongs to the key that opened it.
- The relay keeps the exit socket 30 s after the last carrier left (a client on a new network brings a carrier back to the same socket) and 600 s without packets.

Client behaviour: the stream copy turns on after one second of 15–120 packets a second of at most 500 bytes on average (a call: Opus at 50 pps) and off after 5 s without it; video and downloads stay on the datagram channel alone. When the datagram carrier fails, the stream copy carries the session and the datagram carrier is brought back under the same session. When the datagram channel is down at the start of an association, UDP takes the v1.01 fallback (UoT).

## v1.03: lanes

v1.02 carries a call's voice in the same queues as everything else: one QUIC connection with one DATAGRAM queue (32 frames in quic-go, the writer waits when it is full) for every UDP association of the client, and on the relay the same queue down. A call with video in one socket (Discord, browsers) was not a call to the v1.02 detector at all. Tester tamila-001, 2026-11-09, a Discord call while streaming: rtt p95 414 ms, a 663 ms silence, on a line that is clean directly.

v1.03 keeps the frame (version 1) and the link (`dup=1`) and adds lanes to carriers.

- A carrier attaches to lane 0, every packet, or lane 1, voice: UDP payloads of at most 400 bytes (Opus in SRTP is 60 to 300 bytes, a video packet 1100 to 1200).
- The relay answers every attach with an attach naming the carrier's lane. A v1.02 relay never sends one and ignores the lane byte; a v1.02 client ignores the answer. A client keeps v1.02 behaviour until the answer comes.
- A later attach on the same carrier changes its lane.
- Answers on the relay: voice-sized ones go by the voice carriers and, unless one of them is on the datagram channel, by the whole-lane carriers too; larger ones by the whole-lane carriers, or by every carrier while only voice carriers are left.

Client behaviour with a lanes relay:

- A session is a call after one second with 15–120 voice-sized packets; larger packets beside them do not count. Off after 5 s without it.
- A call's voice-sized packets go by a voice carrier on a second QUIC connection to the relay (its own congestion control and queue, one per client, shared by the client's calls) and by the stream copy (lane 1). Without a second connection, by the datagram carrier.
- Everything else goes by the datagram carrier (lane 0).
- Each carrier has its own send queue (256 frames, the newest lost when full); the application's write never waits for a carrier.
- The datagram carrier fails: the stream copy re-attaches to lane 0 and carries everything until the datagram carrier is back.

Loopback, a call (160 B every 20 ms) with video (about 2.1 Mbit/s, key frames of 40 packets) in one socket over a 2 Mbit/s link, stream channel stalled (`TestCallWithVideoNarrowLink`): v1.02 rtt p95 162–165 ms, worst silence 68–208 ms; v1.03 3 ms and 30 ms.

### The voice connection's sender (2026-11-11, replaced by v1.05)

Replaced by the guard of v1.05 on both datagram connections. It stays only on the rollback (`Sender: bbr`), so that the rollback is the behaviour before v1.05 exactly.

The voice connection carries nothing but calls, paced by the application. Each end gives it its own sender: BBR with at least 256 KiB in flight (`datagram.VoiceWindow`) and no pacing. The client does so when it opens the voice carrier, the relay when a datagram carrier attaches to lane 1. Nothing changes on the wire.

Plain BBR sized the window by the shortest round trip it saw and the rate it delivered. A download on the user's line grows the line's queue: the call no longer fits the window, the delivered rate falls, the window falls with it to four packets (and PROBE_RTT cuts it to four every 10 s), and the call waits seconds in the carrier queues, the newest frames lost there with no loss on the wire. Tester tamila-001, 2026-11-11, Discord on Hikkari while downloading and streaming: the other party as a robot voice; the datagram copy down came 0.5 to 10 s after the stream copy (p50), in one period 16 % of the answers came by the stream copy alone, up the relay saw 7 to 10 % by the stream copy alone, QUIC loss 0. Loopback (`TestCallOnABloatedLine`): 400 voice-sized answers a second down, the downlink queue a sawtooth to 1.5 s with no loss, stream copy stalled: plain BBR 5721 of 6288 answers, 361 later than the line's own delay, the worst 2.7 s; the voice sender 6288 of 6288, none late.

The book counts what a sending end never sent: `dup.full_dgram` and `dup.full_stream`, frames a carrier's queue had no room for (relay: down, client: up).

## v1.05: the datagram channel is a pipe (2026-11-11)

v1.05 changes nothing on the wire: handshake, ALPN, certificate, Chrome's Initial, the Hysteria2 messages, the dup frames and the link stay. It changes when each end sends a datagram it has ready, and what it counts. Each end changes only its own sending, so either end may run it alone: a new relay fixes the direction down for every client, a new client the direction up.

Why. The datagram connections carry only DATAGRAM frames, which QUIC never sends again, and the applications behind them (WebRTC, games, QUIC and HTTP/3 inside the tunnel) run congestion control of their own. Before v1.05 BBR sized each connection's window by the shortest round trip and the rate delivered; a download on the user's line grew the line's queue, the window fell to four packets, and a screen share or a game behind it waited seconds in our queues (the voice lane alone had its own sender since 2026-11-11). Loopback (`TestLiveTrafficOnABloatedLine`, a voice call, a game, a 2.5 Mbit/s screen share and a bulk stream at once, the downlink queue a sawtooth to 2 s): the screen share got 14 to 24 % of its frames, the worst 13 to 17 s late.

### The sender: a guard

Each datagram connection, the whole-lane one and the voice one, at both ends, has the guard (`wire/datagram/guard.go`) for its congestion controller:

- While the path loses nothing, it holds nothing back: no window but memory's (64 MiB in flight), no pacing, no reaction to the round trip.
- It judges each half second of sending. More than 10 % of its bytes lost, out of at least 100 packets, turns a rate limit on: 70 % of the rate the path delivered then, never below 1 Mbit/s (a connection sending less floods no line a call can live on, and the limit never cuts a call that loses by another sender's flood on the same line). Another such half second lowers it the same way. After three half seconds in a row under 2 %, each further one raises it by 3 %, but only while the latest round trip is within 50 ms of the least seen since the limit came on (the queue the excess left on the line has drained); once it is twice what is sent, it goes. The cut is deep and the growth slow because every probe over the line's rate costs everyone on the line a burst of loss: on loopback, a deaf bulk at 130 % of the line beside a call, at 85 % and 10 % a second the call got 82 to 92 % of its packets, at 70 % and 3 % after the hold 93 to 95 % (and 81 % on a loaded machine), growing only on an emptied line 100 % in 24 of 24 runs.
- While limited, the connection's scheduler keeps to the same rate, so a frame over it waits, and ages, in the scheduler and not in QUIC's own queue.

`Sender: bbr` (`datagram.ClientOptions`, `ServiceOptions`, and `client.DatagramOptions`, `server.DatagramOptions` above them) is the rollback: BBR, and the voice connection's sender of 2026-11-11 on the voice lane.

### The scheduler: one per connection, frames by their age

Each QUIC connection has one scheduler (`wire/datagram/sendqueue.go`), the only caller of `SendDatagram`. An association's write puts its message there and returns at once: no write of the application or of the dup layer waits for the wire. The scheduler takes the associations round by round, 1500 bytes each a turn (deficit round robin), so a bulk association does not delay a game's beside it. Right before the wire a message older than `datagram.StaleAfter` (150 ms) is dropped, the oldest first; over 256 KiB waiting the association's oldest are dropped too. A message of several fragments goes, or is dropped, whole; a connection that takes smaller datagrams than thought gets the message again in smaller fragments, and the smaller size from then on. An association's received messages wait in a queue of 256; over it one is dropped.

The stream copy keeps its own queue: a copy that waited longer than `StaleAfter` is dropped before its write. It still waits in TCP's buffer behind the user's download (v1.02's copy decision is the roadmap's 2.4).

### A key's ceiling on the relay

A relay holds each access key's datagrams down to a ceiling, shared by the key's connections (`server.DatagramOptions.KeyCeiling`, bits a second; 100 Mbit/s when zero, none when below zero; `SetKeyCeiling` changes it live, the hub's knob). Over it frames age in the scheduler and are counted stale. The client has no ceiling up: that is its own line.

### Counted (v1.04 summaries)

| Field | Counted by | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dup.stale_dgram` | client (up), relay (down) | frames the scheduler dropped unsent, by age or memory |
| `dup.stale_stream` | client (up), relay (down) | stream copies dropped as too old before their write |
| `dup.rx_drop` | client (down), relay (up) | received messages an association had no room for |
| `dup.queue_ms` | client (up), relay (down) | how long each frame sent waited in the scheduler, ms (`p50`, `p95`, `max`) |
| `quic.<name>.guard_s` | both | seconds the guard held the connection to a rate |

`dup.full_dgram` stays for a frame refused before the scheduler; on a live carrier it is 0. The associations of v1.01 (no dup) are counted by the scheduler but not yet summarised. QUIC's own receive queue (128) drops without a count.

## Admission

The fleet's admission probe (hikkari-control `cmd/entry-prober`) checks a relay as clients use it: the stream channel through Chromium, the datagram channel by a plain Hysteria2 association, and, since 2026-11-09, a dup session as a `dup=1` client opens it (this package's client with a voice connection, the stream copy through Chromium): a call of 250 × 160 B every 20 ms to a STUN server. The row says whether the relay answered the attach (lanes), how much of the call came back, how many answers each carrier brought and how many sources the far end saw.

## v1.04: observability (agreed 2026-11-10)

v1.04 changes nothing on the wire. Each end counts what it already sees of its sessions and reports a summary: the relay to the hub in its pulse, the client in its log. No summary carries an address, a destination or a key; the relay's summaries are keyed by the key's account on the hub side only (the pulse already carries per-key byte totals).

### What is counted, and where

| Field | Counted by | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stream channel: `rtt`, `rttvar`, `retrans`, `lost`, `cwnd`, `bytes_out`, `bytes_in` | relay | Linux `TCP_INFO` of the TCP socket under the HTTP/2 connection, sampled once a minute and at its end |
| datagram channel: `rtt` (smoothed), `rtt_min`, `packets_out`, `packets_lost`, `bytes_out`, `bytes_lost` | relay and client | QUIC connection statistics, for the whole-lane and the voice connection apart |
| end of a connection: `eof`, `reset`, `idle`, `revoked`, `relay_stop`, `error` | relay and client | where the connection's serve loop returns |
| dup up (relay), dup down (client): `fresh`, `first_datagram`, `first_stream`, `only_datagram`, `only_stream`, `lead` | relay and client | the dup window remembers, per number, which carrier brought the first copy and whether the other one came; `only_stream` is a packet the stream copy saved; `lead` is how much earlier the first copy was |
| call detector: per call, `seconds`, `pps_up`, `pps_down`, `bytes_mean`, `voice_share` | client | the v1.03 detector, from on to off; a call shorter than 10 s or one-sided (`pps_down` under a third of `pps_up`) is a likely false call |
| trunk: per member end, `lived`, `why` (`stalled`, `broken`), `rtt`, `retrans` at the end; `resent`, `epoch` | entry and exit | the trunk session's counters plus `TCP_INFO` of the member's socket on the exit |
| path: `direct` or `entry` | hub | from which link the account's key came by; the client does not know |

A number in a summary is a count since the last summary, or a distribution of the period as `p50`, `p95`, `max`.

### Relay → hub

Every pulse (30 s) carries `hikkari`: periods by `<path>/<key>` and `hikkariS`, the seconds they cover. `path` is `direct` for a client's own connection and `entry` for one the cascade's trunk brought on loopback (its TCP and QUIC numbers are the box's, not the path's); the trunk's members are `trunk/`. A period holds the stream connections by `TCP_INFO` (`tcp`), the CONNECT tunnels by how they ended (`tunnels`), the datagram channel's QUIC connections (`quic.datagram`) and the dup up counters (`dup`). A pulse the hub did not take keeps its periods for the next one. The hub replaces the key by its account and writes every number as a row of ClickHouse `hikkari_obs`.

### Client → hub

The client engine writes `hikkari period s=<seconds> <period>` to its log every 10 minutes and when the outbound closes: `quic.datagram` and `quic.voice`, `dup` (the down direction) and `calls`. The app files the line under source `hikkari` and sends it with the rest of the log; the hub writes it to the same table under the install.

### Not in v1.04

The client's view of the stream channel (Chromium's own RTT and losses) is not visible to Go code. If the relay's `TCP_INFO` does not answer a question, a later version may add a service stream inside the Naive tunnel (a reserved CONNECT name, rare small frames at random intervals and sizes); not before the data says it is needed.
